Why this BU is structurally different
Mining is not a harder version of Coinhold — it's a different model entirely. Non-overlapping audience, capital-dependent decision cycle (45–120 days), and revenue comes from a pool fee on contributed hashrate — not take-rate on trading. A deposit-CPA framework applied to Mining produces numbers that are wrong in every direction.
The core principle
CPA in isolation is a vanity metric. What matters is whether blended CAC pays back before the user churns, given each country's ARPPU for this specific BU and positioning. A $15 CPA with 4-month payback (AR Coinhold) is fundamentally different from a $15 CPA with 9-month payback. The denominator is ARPPU — which varies 3× across LATAM at the same CPA.

Why three tracks and not one
Track A optimizes for volume at the lowest CPA. Track B optimizes for quality — users who activate multiple products from day one have materially higher ARPPU, making the higher CPA pay back faster. Track C is a different business entirely: 45–120 day funnel, lead model, different audience, different channels, different revenue structure. Running all three from one CPA target produces the right total spend and the wrong users.
Terminology reference — CPL · MQL · SQL
CPL — Cost Per Lead
A metric, not a lead type. How much you paid to acquire one lead regardless of quality.
CPL = Spend ÷ Total leads
AR $9.54 · PY $12.75 · BR $3.29
MQL — Marketing Qualified Lead
Lead that shows enough interest or fits the right profile to be worth tracking — but not yet validated as sales-ready. Marketing owns this stage.
For EMCD: anyone who submits the pool registration form (leads_reg)
SQL — Sales Qualified Lead
Lead validated as genuinely ready for a sales conversation — meets criteria (has equipment, has hashrate) or shows strong engagement. Sales owns this stage.
For EMCD: sql_act column — 24 AR · 25 PY · 12 BR
The current problem: no qualification step exists between MQL and SQL today. Marketing dumps all leads into the same bucket and sales figures out who's real — which is why the lead-to-SQL rate is only 9% in AR/PY and 1.5% in BR. The two-bucket flow below solves this at the source.

Diagnosis for our current Mining Pool Leads performance
The CPL is cheap across all three markets ($3–13). That is not the problem. The problem is the lead-to-SQL conversion rate — 9% in AR/PY, 1.5% in Brazil — which inflates the real cost of acquiring a pool-ready user to $107–220. The $35–80 CPL estimates in the original framework were correct for MQL. The CPA SQL target of $70–100 is the number to drive toward — achievable by lifting the lead-to-SQL rate through a structured nurture flow, not by spending more on paid.

Brazil anomaly: 804 leads at $3.29 but only 12 SQLs (1.5%) signals Meta is finding cheap clickers, not real miners. Fix: add a qualifying question upfront in the lead form ("Do you have mining equipment?" / "What is your current hashrate?"). This will raise CPL to ~$8–12 but should multiply the SQL rate to 8–12%, cutting CPA SQL from $220 to $70–90.

Why this changes the CPA SQL dramatically
Today marketing sends all 269 leads (AR) to sales indiscriminately — 245 aren't ready, sales burns time, only 24 convert. With the two-bucket split: Bucket A leads (estimated 20–30% of total) get sales contact in 24–48h and convert at 40–50%. Bucket B engaged leads (another 15–20%) convert at 15–20% after 7-day nurture. Blended SQL rate goes from 9% → 25–30%, cutting CPA SQL from $107 to $35–50 in AR/PY — without increasing paid spend.

The fix is in the lead form, not in nurture length. Two qualifying questions upfront — "Do you currently have mining equipment?" and "What is your estimated hashrate?" — split the buckets at source and give sales the right leads at the right time. Marketing owns lead quality. Sales only touches Bucket A and engaged Bucket B.

Peru entry — two viable models
Offshore model (Binance / OKX playbook): operate without local structure. Access PEN via P2P or third-party payment processors — you never hold PEN directly. Binance, OKX, and Bybit all run Peruvian campaigns from offshore with no SBS authorization and have faced zero enforcement to date. Higher funnel friction (P2P deposit step) and an advertising grey zone that is currently unenforced but real on paper. Lower upfront cost, higher CPA.

Local partnership model (Lemon playbook): partner with an SBS-licensed EEDE (e.g. G-Money SA) for the PEN/fiat layer + external VASP license (El Salvador or Argentina CNV) for the crypto layer. Unlocks seamless Yape/Plin on-ramps as a first-party wallet feature — no P2P middleman. Full paid media freedom. Lemon reached 1M users and 150K Visa cards with this structure. Higher upfront BD investment, lower CPA.

The choice is a product decision, not just a compliance one. Offshore is viable to test. The EEDE partnership is the right move when frictionless PEN deposits become a meaningful CPA lever — which they will at scale.

The Brazil correction
BCB Resolutions 519-521 explicitly exclude transaction validation and block creation from the PSAV framework. Pool operations are NOT regulated. Stop reporting CPA per country in isolation
The dashboard needs to show blended CAC, ARPPU, and payback period per country per track. A higher CPA that buys a faster-paying user (Track B) is better than a lower CPA that buys a slower-paying one (Track A). The number to manage to is payback months, not CPA.
Immediate action
🏛️
Choose your Peru entry model before spending
Two viable paths exist. Offshore (Binance/OKX model): no local structure, PEN access via P2P, currently unenforced advertising grey zone — viable to test, higher funnel friction, higher CPA. Local partnership (Lemon model): SBS-licensed EEDE (G-Money SA) for Yape/Plin + external VASP license for crypto — seamless deposits, full ad freedom, validated at 1M users, lower CPA but requires upfront BD investment. The decision is a product one: offshore works to test; the EEDE partnership is the right move when frictionless PEN deposits become a meaningful CPA lever.
Decide before first spend
🤝
Coordinate both BUs in Paraguay
Paraguay is Tier 5 for Coinhold and Tier 1 for Mining. Running them independently wastes the overlap. Mining BU awareness spend on X, TikTok, and programmatic builds brand recognition that reduces Coinhold's acquisition cost in the same market. Budgets and content calendars should be coordinated.
Structural decision
⏱️
Mining BU needs 60-day view-through attribution
Last-click makes TikTok and X look like they generate zero conversions. Without a 60-day view-through window, the growth team will cut awareness spend within 30 days of launch, starving the funnel. This must be configured before the first Track C dollar is spent.
Before first spend
🔧
Build the MQL→SQL nurture flow before scaling Mining paid
Today AR/PY convert 9% of leads to SQLs — the other 91% disappear with no follow-up. Brazil converts 1.5%. Without a nurture flow, you're paying $107–220 per SQL when you should be paying $70–100. The infrastructure needed: 14-day email sequence (education → calculator → social proof), intent triggers (3+ email opens, calculator use, setup page visit), and direct outreach (WhatsApp/email) for qualified leads. This is the highest-ROI action for Mining BU — it reduces CPA SQL by 35–40% without increasing paid spend. Brazil additionally needs a qualifying question in the lead form before any spend increase.
Before scaling paid
🧱
Track B and Track A must run in separate campaigns
If Track B runs in the same campaign as Track A with the same deposit target, Meta collapses it into the cheapest depositor — the Track A profile. Track B stops existing in practice. Separate campaign structures, separate lookalike audiences, separate CPA benchmarks. Ops decision, not a strategy debate.
Campaign setup
🇦🇷
Argentina is priority for both BUs — two actions needed
Coinhold BU — accelerate PSAV / CNV registration: Argentina is the #1 Coinhold market with the best payback in scope. CNV registration is mandatory and the incoming regulatory framework is tightening. Being registered early signals legitimacy to users and partners, smooths the transition to any future SBS-equivalent regime, and removes the compliance overhang from paid media and BD conversations. Every quarter without it is a risk accumulating on the best market.

Mining BU — watch energy costs quarterly: Argentina is Tier 1 for Mining based on cheap provincial energy (San Juan, Mendoza, Neuquén). But Milei's deregulation is dismantling those subsidies. Set a quarterly review: if energy costs in key provinces move payback above 14 months, Paraguay absorbs the Argentina allocation.
